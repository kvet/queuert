A job is one unit of work that runs once to completion, and a chain is the sequence of jobs that a handler hands on to each other with `continueWith`. The chain completes when its last job completes without continuing.

Like a Promise chain, which is identified by its first promise, a Queuert chain is identified by its first job, the head job. Unlike a Promise chain, it is stored in your database, so it survives restarts and runs on any worker.

## Identity

The head job's `id` is the chain's id: `job.id === job.chainId`. A continuation job has its own `id` and shares the head's `chainId`. Each job also carries `chainIndex`, its position in the chain: `0` for the head, then one more for each continuation.

`createChain` returns the chain with `chain.id` set to the head job's id. The job currently at the end of the chain is the tail.

## Storage

There is no separate chain table. A chain's own facts (type name, status, deduplication key, trace context, completion time, whether other jobs depend on it) live on its head row in the `job` table, and continuations are further rows in the same table. Dependencies between chains are rows in the `job_blocker` junction table.

## Status

A job moves through `blocked` (only when it has unfinished blockers), `pending`, `running` and `completed`. A chain is `running` until its last job completes, then `completed`. Both use the same words, so a status read from a job or from a chain means the same thing.

## Blockers

A chain can wait for other chains to complete before it starts.

Blockers are declared at the type level and passed in the `blockers` array when creating a chain, either as chains or by id. A `continueWith` outcome takes `blockers` the same way, next to its `typeName` and `input`, so a continuation can wait on other chains too. The main job starts `blocked` while any blocker chain is still running, otherwise `pending`, and becomes `pending` once all blockers complete. The [Job blockers](/queuert/guides/job-blockers/) guide shows how to declare and read them.
