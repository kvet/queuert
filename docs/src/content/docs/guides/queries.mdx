`listChains` and `listJobs` route queries to status-specific indexes based on the `status` and `orderBy` combination. ## Locked reads

`getChain`, `getChains`, `getJob`, `getJobs`, `getChainTailJob` and `getChainTailJobs` take `lock: "exclusive"`, which locks the rows they match until the transaction ends: a chain read locks the chain's head job, a job read locks the job and its chain's head job, and a tail read locks the chain's head job and its tail job. Inside one transaction, read with a lock, check the result, then write with [`applyJobOutcome`](../workerless-outcomes/), and no worker or other caller changes or deletes those rows in between: another caller that locks the same rows waits for your transaction to end. A tail read that waits on a transaction that continues the chain returns the continuation. On PostgreSQL at REPEATABLE READ or SERIALIZABLE, a locked read of a row another transaction changed after your transaction's snapshot fails with a serialization failure for you to retry; see [Isolation level](/queuert/integrations/state-adapters/postgres/#isolation-level). On MySQL and MariaDB, a locked read can fail with a deadlock or a lock wait timeout; see [Lock errors](/queuert/integrations/state-adapters/mysql/#lock-errors). On Redis nothing waits: the transaction that commits second runs again; see [Locks](/queuert/integrations/state-adapters/redis/#locks).

<ExampleCode example="guides" file="src/queries.ts" region="locked-reads" />

`lock: "exclusive"` requires a transaction context with every field the provider's context type requires, so a locked read without one does not compile. A read that matches nothing locks nothing, so a lock does not serialize a "create if absent" against a concurrent create; close that race with [deduplication](../deduplication/). Keep locked transactions short, since workers cannot start the locked jobs until they end.

To own a chain for the rest of a transaction, read it with `getChainTailJob({ ...txCtx, chainId, lock: "exclusive" })`. It locks the chain's head job, which is the chain's lock, and its current tail, so no worker or caller completes, continues, reschedules or deletes the chain, and no new chain starts waiting on it, until your transaction ends. Lists need no lock of their own: once the chain is locked, `listChainJobs` and `listBlockedJobs` for it read rows that only a holder of that lock can change. `getChain` with a lock takes only the head: the chain cannot complete, be deleted or gain blocked jobs, but its tail can still continue.

Queuert takes row locks in one order, and a transaction that follows it waits instead of deadlocking:

- Lock every chain you need in one read, since a read locks its chains' head jobs in ID order before any other job.
- Lock the chains a chain waits on before the chain itself, the way completing a chain locks it and then the jobs that wait on it.

A worker that completes a job locks the job before its chain's head, so a locked read can still deadlock with a completion of the same chain. The database then aborts one of the two transactions; retry it on a deadlock error.
