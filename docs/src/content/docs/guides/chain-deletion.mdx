Chains can be deleted using `deleteChains` (plural) or `deleteChain` (singular). All jobs in the chain, its head job and continuations, are removed together:

<ExampleCode example="guides" file="src/chain-deletion.ts" region="delete" />

When a deleted chain has a running job, its attempt handler's `applyJobOutcome` throws `JobNotFoundError`. The attempt is also notified as lost, and its `job.signal` aborts once the notification reaches the worker runner, which happens when the runner was built from the deleting client or both share a notify adapter. If the attempt is inside a [fenced](/queuert/how-it-works/job-processing/#fencejob) transaction, the delete waits for it to end, except on Redis, where the transaction that commits second runs again rather than waiting.

Use `deleteChain` to target a single chain — it returns the deleted chain or `undefined` if no chain with that ID exists. `deleteChains` silently skips missing IDs and returns the chains that were actually deleted. Both calls are idempotent: deleting the same chain again returns `undefined` from `deleteChain` and `[]` from `deleteChains`:

Before deleting, `deleteChains` locks the target chains and checks whether any of them is still a blocker: a chain with `blocking: true` has at least one job waiting on it. Such a chain is rejected with `BlockerReferenceError`, whose `chainIds` lists the blocking chains, and nothing is deleted, even when the waiting jobs belong to chains in the same call. This prevents orphaning blocked chains that would never unblock. Deleting a `fetch-data` chain is rejected while `generate-report` still waits on it:

## Deleting a chain with its blockers

Deletion never follows blocker relationships on its own. To remove a chain together with the chains it waits on, collect its blocker chains with `getJobBlockers`, delete the chain, and then delete the blockers in a second `deleteChains` call in the same transaction. Deleting the dependent chain removes its blocker references, which clears `blocking` on each blocker it was the last to reference:

<ExampleCode example="guides" file="src/chain-deletion.ts" region="co-delete" />

`getJobBlockers` takes a job ID: a chain's head job has the chain's ID, and a continuation declared with its own `blockers` has to be queried separately (list the chain's jobs with `listChainJobs`). Repeat the lookup on each blocker chain to reach blockers of blockers. If a blocker is still a blocker of another job — for example, one shared with another report — the second call throws `BlockerReferenceError` and deletes nothing; leave that chain out, or delete its other dependents first.

## What gets deleted

Given a list of `ids`, the operation deletes all jobs in each chain (every job where `job.chainId` matches a provided ID, including the head and continuations), together with the blocker references those jobs hold on other chains. A chain that other jobs still wait on is never deleted — it makes the whole call fail.

The runnable code is in [examples/guides/src/chain-deletion.ts](https://github.com/kvet/queuert/blob/main/examples/guides/src/chain-deletion.ts). Related: [Tx hooks](../tx-hooks/) and [Job blockers](../job-blockers/).
