A job can run more than once: an attempt that throws or whose worker dies is retried. Writes to your database stay safe when they share a transaction with `applyJobOutcome`, because the job's result and its completion then commit together, exactly once.

The previous lesson showed what happens to a failed attempt: the job goes back to `pending` and the handler runs again from the top. Anything the first attempt committed on its own stays committed, so a handler that updates a row in one transaction and completes the job in another applies that update once per attempt. Spreading the same `txCtx` into your SQL and into `applyJobOutcome` closes the gap: if the attempt fails before the commit, the update rolls back with the completion, and once it commits the job is completed and never runs again.

This covers writes to your own database. A call to another service, such as a payment API, cannot join the transaction, so it can run again on a retry: pass `job.id` as its idempotency key so the service applies it once. [Job processing reliability](../../how-it-works/processing-reliability/) walks through every place an attempt can fail.

The `reserve-stock` job below takes 2 lamps from a stock of 10, but runs its `UPDATE` before the transaction that completes the job, and its first attempt crashes in between. The retry reserves the lamps again and the stock ends at 6.

<TryIt lesson="transactional-completion">run the `UPDATE` inside the transaction with `sql(txCtx)`, so the crashed attempt's reservation rolls back and the stock ends at 8.</TryIt>
